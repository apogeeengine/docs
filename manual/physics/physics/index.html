    Physics¶

    Apogee.Physics is Jolt Physics for the ECS: entities get bodies, the simulation steps at a fixed rate, and the entities' transforms follow. The raw library is in Native libraries; this is what the engine makes of it. In a game, scripts see the world as the global physics.

    local Transform = world:component("Transform")
local ground = world:create_entity("ground")
world:set(ground, Transform, { Position = Vector3(0, -1, 0), Rotation = Quat.identity, Scale = Vector3(1, 1, 1) })
local floor = BodyDesc.new()
floor.shape = PhysicsShape.box(Vector3(100, 1, 100))        -- half extents
floor.motion = "static"
physics:add_body(ground, floor)

local crate = world:create_entity("crate")
world:set(crate, Transform, { Position = Vector3(0, 5, 0), Rotation = Quat.identity, Scale = Vector3(1, 1, 1) })
local body = BodyDesc.new()
body.shape = PhysicsShape.box(Vector3(0.5, 0.5, 0.5))
body.mass = 20
body.events = true                                           -- report its contacts
physics:add_body(crate, body)

physics.contact_began:connect(function(a, b, point, normal) print("hit", a, b, point) end)
local hit, entity, point, normal, distance = physics:raycast(Vector3(0, 10, 0), Vector3(0, -1, 0), 50)

    The world and its frame¶

    PhysicsWorld(ecsWorld, options) makes one simulation for an ECS world; a game session makes it for you. Call Update(deltaSeconds) once a frame, before World.Progress (ScriptSession.Progress does): the physics then writes the transforms of its bodies, the frame's systems and scripts see them, and the world's transform system composes the WorldTransforms as usual.

    • A fixed step, StepRate times a second (64 in the tests, 60 by default). Update adds the frame's time to an accumulator and runs as many steps as fit, at most MaxSubsteps (4): a slow frame loses time instead of spiralling. The results depend on the step rate, not on the frame rate or the number of threads.
    • stepping (Stepping in C#) is raised before each fixed step. Anything that must act once per step, vehicle controls above all, connects there, not to a per-frame callback.
    • Interpolation (Interpolate, on by default): the entity of a moving dynamic body shows the pose between its last two steps by how far the accumulator is into the next one, so motion is smooth at any frame rate, at the price of showing the past by up to one step. physics:pose(entity) is the exact pose of the last step. A body that comes to rest shows exactly where it is, not a blend.
    • Determinism: the library is built so that the same inputs give the same bits on every OS (see Native libraries), whatever ThreadCount is.
    • Options: MaxBodies (10 240), MaxBodyPairs, MaxContactConstraints, TempBufferBytes (32 MiB), Gravity, VelocitySteps 10, PositionSteps 2, EventCapacity (4096). These are the old project's settings.

    Bodies¶

    A BodyDesc says what a body is: its shape, motion, mass (or a density, with the shape's volume), friction, restitution, damping, gravity_factor, layers and mask, whether it is a sensor, whether it reports events, allow_sleeping, bullet (sweep the whole path, so a fast body cannot tunnel through a thin wall) and its starting velocities. One description can make any number of bodies. physics:add_body(entity, desc) puts the body where the entity's transform is now; the entity gets a Transform if it has none, and a Body component (Id, Slot, Motion). Removing the component (physics:remove_body) or destroying the entity removes the body.

    motion Meaning
    static Never moves (ground, walls). physics:teleport moves it, slowly.
    kinematic The game moves it: it goes to where its entity is by the end of each frame's steps, and pushes dynamic bodies (doors, lifts, platforms).
    dynamic The simulation moves it, and its entity follows.

    Shapes¶

    A PhysicsShape is made with a static function and shared by any number of bodies: box(halfExtents), sphere(radius), capsule(halfHeight, radius) and cylinder(halfHeight, radius) (along Y), convex_hull(points), mesh(vertices, indices) and height_field(heights, size, offset, scale) (static and kinematic bodies only), compound(children, positions, rotations), offset(shape, position, rotation) (moves the shape inside its own space, so a box can stand on the entity's origin) and scaled(shape, scale). Jolt counts references, so a shape that is dropped lives on in the bodies that use it. volume, center_of_mass and is_valid_scale(scale) say what it is. Dimensions are checked: a zero radius or a mesh index past the vertices is an error with the reason.

    Scale. The entity's world scale is applied to the shape when the body is made (a scale a shape cannot take, such as a stretched sphere, is an error). Changing the scale later does not change the body: make it again.

    How a body fits the entity tree¶

    Transform is local to the parent and WorldTransform is computed top-down (see Math). A body takes its world pose from World.ComputeWorldTransform(entity) when it is made, and a dynamic body writes back a local Transform: its world pose from Jolt, taken into the space of its parent (parentWorld⁻¹ × pose, with the position subtraction done in double). The scale is left alone. So a crate under a moving platform entity falls in the world's space, and its local transform changes to match. A kinematic body reads its entity's world transform. The rule that keeps this simple: a body's ancestors must not be dynamic bodies (add_body refuses); a parent that is not a body, or is static or kinematic, is fine.

    Layers¶

    A body is in some of 14 collision layers (layers, a bit each: 1 is the default layer) and collides with the layers in its mask (all by default). Two bodies collide when each is in a layer the other's mask holds. Two more bits are the world's own and sort bodies into three broad-phase trees (static, sensor, moving); Jolt never collides two bodies that are both static or kinematic. The native layer builds the object layer from them, and the query filters (mask, ignore) run natively too: see Native/jolt/apogee_jolt.cpp.

    Events¶

    Bodies that have events (and every sensor) report to the world's event queue (apogee_jolt), which is drained after each step on the main thread:

    Signal Arguments
    contact_began the two entities, the contact point (a position) and the normal
    contact_ended the two entities (also when one of them goes to sleep: Jolt ends the contacts of a sleeping body)
    sensor_entered, sensor_exited the sensor's entity, then the other
    body_woke, body_slept the entity

    A sensor (sensor = true) does not collide and is not pushed; it reports the bodies inside it. Events a step produced beyond EventCapacity are dropped and counted (dropped_events). A signal handler runs while the world is not inside Jolt, so it may add or remove bodies.

    Queries¶

    All positions are in the world, in double precision, and layers are a mask; ignore is an entity the query passes through. Sensors are never hit.

    • raycast(origin, direction, maxDistance, mask?, ignore?) returns hit, entity, point, normal, distance.
    • cast_sphere(origin, direction, radius, maxDistance, mask?, ignore?) returns the same for the first body a swept sphere touches (point is on the body, distance how far the sphere got).
    • overlap_sphere(center, radius, mask?, ignore?) and overlap_box(center, rotation, halfExtents, mask?, ignore?) return the entities of the bodies that touch the shape, each once.

    Bodies from code¶

    get_linear_velocity, set_linear_velocity, get_angular_velocity, set_angular_velocity, add_force (newtons, for the next step only: call it every step), add_torque, add_impulse (newton seconds), wake, is_awake, pose (the exact pose), teleport(entity, position, rotation) (body and entity at once, no blend).

    Vehicles¶

    physics:create_vehicle and physics:create_hitch: see Vehicles and hitches. BodyDesc.center_of_mass and physics:get_mass(entity) are for them.

    Characters¶

    physics:create_character(entity, height, radius, layers?, mask?) makes a character controller (Jolt's CharacterVirtual): a capsule whose feet are at the entity's position that slides along walls, walks up stairs (steps up to 0.4 m) and slopes (up to max_slope_degrees, read-only, 50), sticks to the ground going down and stands on moving platforms. It is not a rigid body: dynamic bodies are pushed by it and do not push it. Set desired_velocity (the horizontal part counts; gravity and jumps decide the vertical one), call jump(speed) (from the ground only), read is_on_ground, ground_velocity, velocity and position. It steps with the world, and its entity follows, blended like a dynamic body. teleport(position) and set_rotation(rotation) move it; destroying it stops the following.

    Far from the origin¶

    Jolt is built with double positions, so a body, a ray or a character is as exact 10 000 km out as at the origin; the tests run the same scenes at x = 1e7. Rotations, scales, velocities and forces are floats. Scripts see positions as vectors, narrowed to float (see Math): a script that works that far out keeps its coordinates relative to something near.

    Pausing¶

    physics.paused = true stops the world: Update adds no time and runs no step, so bodies stay where they are, keep their velocities, and resume from there when it is false again. A pause menu sets it.

    Debug drawing¶

    physics.debug_draw = true (or the Physics shapes checkbox in the dev overlay's Debug section) draws the world over the picture: every body's shape as wireframe, colored by what it is, the joints and drawbars, a vehicle's wheels and suspension, and the capsules of characters. In C#, PhysicsWorld.DrawDebug(DebugDraw draw, PhysicsDebugOptions? options, Double3 camera) adds the same to any DebugDraw; PhysicsDebugOptions chooses what: shapes (solid or wireframe), bounding boxes, centers of mass, velocities, constraints and their limits, characters. camera picks the level of detail of round shapes.

    Jolt draws, and our C API only collects: aj_world_draw_debug runs Jolt's DebugRendererSimple into one process-wide collector (Jolt allows one debug renderer at a time; it is made on first use and freed by aj_shutdown), and aj_debug_lines / aj_debug_triangles hand its arrays over, so no managed code runs inside Jolt's drawing. DebugLine and DebugTriangle have the layout of the C structs (a test pins it), so the arrays are copied as they are. A character is not a body, so aj_character_draw_debug draws its capsule separately. Jolt's wireframe is the edges of the shape's triangle mesh (a box is its twelve triangles, so 36 lines), not just its outline.

    Not done¶

    Constraints (joints beyond the hitches), soft bodies, ragdolls, and changing a body's shape or scale after it is made. The Jolt library has all of them in its C API (Apogee.Native.Jolt).

    Tests¶

    Tests/Apogee.Physics.Tests covers falling and resting, double precision 10 000 km out, parent spaces, kinematic carrying, scale, teleport, impulses and mass, the fixed step and interpolation, queries, events, sensors, layers, every shape, the character, debug drawing (the layout of its structs, a box's lines at their place 1 000 km out, solid and wireframe, bounding boxes, the character's capsule), and that the result is the same with 1 and 4 threads. Tests/Luau/physics.test.luau runs the script surface in the NativeAOT binary too.
